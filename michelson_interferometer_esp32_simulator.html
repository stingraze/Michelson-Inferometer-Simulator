Physics & Engineering Principles

1. Wave Interference Equation

When the 650nm laser beam is split and recombined, the light intensity $I(x, \Delta d)$ at detector coordinate $x$ is given by:

I(x) = I₀ · [1 + cos( (4π · Δd / λ) + (2π · x / Λ) )]

Where $\Delta d$ is the path difference between the fixed and movable mirrors, $\lambda = 650\text{ nm}$, and $\Lambda = \frac{\lambda}{2\theta}$ is the spatial fringe pitch created by mirror tilt $\theta$.

2. Why Use 4 CdS Photoresistors?

A single photoresistor only measures light intensity at one point in space. By arranging 4 CdS sensors in a linear array across the interference spot:

  • Quadrature & Direction Tracking: Spacing sensors at sub-fringe intervals allows determination of the direction of mirror motion (forward vs. backward) from phase leads/lags.
  • Spatial Fringe Profiling: Directly measures the spatial fringe pitch $\Lambda$ and tilt angle $\theta$ without mechanically sweeping the detector.
  • Common-Mode Noise Cancellation: Helps isolate true optical phase shifts from global laser power fluctuations or ambient light changes.

3. ESP32 Circuit & CdS Response Lag

Each CdS cell is wired in a voltage divider circuit with a $10\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor connected to ESP32 ADC pins (GPIO 34, 35, 32, 33):

V_out = 3.3V · [ R_fixed / (R_CdS + R_fixed) ]

CdS photoresistors rely on bulk semiconductor electron-hole recombination, introducing a temporal response delay ($\sim 20\text{ to }50\text{ ms}$). This simulator accurately models this thermal photoconductive inertia!
